    Method

    How to read the three numbers. All numbers count Democratic seats in the U.S. House. Proportional gives each state's seats in proportion to its two-party vote. Neutral baseline is the median of maps per state drawn by a computer that follows each state's rules but ignores party. Enacted is what the maps used on November 3, 2026 produce with the same votes. The difference between the first two is the geographic effect; the difference between the last two is the drawing effect. An effect shows its direction: R+6 means 6 seats toward Republicans, D+3 means 3 seats toward Democrats.

    Why neutral maps are not proportional. Each district elects one winner, so the party with more votes in a state usually wins a larger share of its seats than of its votes. Democratic voters also live closer together, mostly in cities, so neutral maps often put many of them in a few districts that Democrats win by large margins. The geographic effect is the sum of both.

    Votes. We use the 2016 and 2020 presidential results added together, by voting district (by census tract in California, Hawaii, Maine and Oregon, by county in Iowa and West Virginia; West Virginia uses 2020 only), as prepared by the ALARM Project. The enacted seats come from assigning these votes to the 2026 districts, not from House results. With these votes the enacted seats can differ from what happened in 2024.

    The Voting Rights Act. The law can require districts where a minority group forms a large share of the voters. In seat counts such districts can look like packing. ALARM's neutral maps follow the Voting Rights Act as courts applied it before Louisiana v. Callais (April 2026). Where a map adopted after that ruling removed such districts, the change shows up in the drawing effect. The notes per state say what ALARM did there.

    Neutral maps that keep old district cores. In Kansas and Nebraska, ALARM's neutral maps keep the cores of the districts drawn after the 2010 census. In Louisiana, New Mexico and Utah they keep those of the 2021 or 2022 map. In New Mexico, and largely in Louisiana, that pulls the drawing effect toward zero.

    The national band. The national neutral baseline combines the states as if their neutral maps were drawn independently. The national effects therefore differ slightly from the sum of the state effects.

    Extensions. The majority threshold shifts every district's Democratic share by the same amount until Democrats win of the districts; the six states with one seat stay with the party that won them in 2016 and 2020. Competitive districts have a Democratic share between 45% and 55%. Redraws compare the 2026 maps with the maps of the 2024 election (U.S. Census Bureau, 119th Congress). "Adopted by" says which body adopted the map and which party, if any, could adopt it alone. The Senate block gives both seats of a state to the party that won its presidential vote in 2016 and 2020 together. A majority is 51 of 100 seats. At 50 to 50 the vice president, a Republican since January 2025, decides, so Democrats need states. Real Senate races also depend on the candidates, and only a third of the seats are up at each election.

    Known limits.

    • The smallest vote units are voting districts in 38 states, census tracts in California, Hawaii, Maine and Oregon, and counties in Iowa and West Virginia, as in ALARM's map files. Where such a unit crosses a district line, we split its votes over its census blocks by voting-age population. In the four tract states the split follows the block votes in ALARM's census-2020 files.
    • In California, ALARM's map file has no votes for two census tracts in Modoc and Siskiyou counties. ALARM's census-2020 block file gives them about 4,100 votes in 2016 and 2020 together. We follow the map file, so all three numbers use the same votes.
    • In Michigan, ALARM's map file leaves out 38 voting districts that cover water. ALARM's census-2020 data gives them 17,273 votes, which we do not count. In Ohio the map file leaves out 4 such districts, which have no votes.
    • ALARM's census-2020 block file for Maine is from October 2025 and spreads the votes over the tracts differently from the map file. We use it only to split each tract's votes over its blocks; the votes per tract come from the map file.
    • For New Jersey, ALARM's map file has the 2020 results from before ALARM revised its census-2020 file for that state. We follow the map file.
    • For a voting district or county without voting-age population, we split its votes equally over its blocks. On the 2026 maps such units with votes cross a district line in eight states, with about 4,500 votes in total.
    • We do not check the enacted maps for equal population. Nevada and California count prisoners differently from the census when they redistrict.
    • A district number can mean a different area on another map, as in Utah, so district numbers cannot be compared between maps.
    • With the 2016 and 2020 presidential votes, TX-15, TX-28 and TX-34 count as Democratic districts. On the same lines, all three voted Republican for president in 2024, according to the Texas Legislative Council.
